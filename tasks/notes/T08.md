# T08 notes: execution console

## Done
- `scenes/ExecutionConsole.tscn` / `scripts/execution/ExecutionConsole.gd`: the EXECUTION half, built on Amit's mock-up `design/execution_screen.png`, which is stretched to 640×400. No `STUB(T08)` remains.
- One script per panel, each with `show_frame(frame: TelemetryFrame)`: `ClockPanel`, `ObjectivesPanel`, `AlarmPanel`, `RadarPanel`, `TemperaturePanel`, `AnglesPanel`, `AltitudePanel`, `DeltaVPanel`, `AccelerationPanel`. The five small instruments share the base `InstrumentPanel`.
- Sensor states: NO SENSOR / NO SIGNAL / the value with its unit, through `ReadingFormat.reading_text`.
- Radar: contacts are placed at their bearing and range. Level 0 is a blip; level 1 adds the distance; level 2 adds an outline, the type, the distance and the closing speed. NO SENSOR when no radar is installed.
- Angles: θ (attitude) and γ (flight-path angle) in degrees, each with its own sensor state. Acceleration is in g, with "ENGINE ON" or "coasting".
- Alarm: the lamp flashes (0.5 s on, 0.5 s off) while `active_alarms` is not empty, and the readable alarm texts sit beside it. The `alert_raised` message stays until the player dismisses it, with OK or by resuming the flight (see deviations). OK does not resume the flight.
- Time controls (II, 1x, 10x, 100x, 1000x, from `ALLOWED_TIME_SCALES`) highlight `frame.time_scale`. Pause calls `FlightSimulation.pause`; the others call `set_time_scale`.
- Left column: mission time, time to the next pulse ("none" when no pulse is left), fuel left, science data generated and delivered, and the objective list (target orbit, science mission, accepted side quests). It refreshes on `GameState.mission_changed`.
- The self-destruct button: the first press arms it ("SURE?"), the second shows one of four jokes. It never touches the flight.
- `ScreenLayout.CONSOLE_SWITCH_RECT` is left empty.
- Debug panel (`DebugTruthPanel`, debug builds only, F3): the true flight state from `FlightSimulation`, with no sensor in between. It shows time, altitude, speed, flight-path and polar angles, eccentricity, periapsis, apoapsis, period, mass, propellant, stages, working instruments and threats. It covers the left column while shown. **It is a deliberate exception** to "read nothing else from `FlightSimulation`", allowed only because players never see it: release builds cannot show it, and it starts hidden.
- Demo: `scenes/development/T08_demo.tscn` steps through eleven scenarios with the < and > buttons. Under the buttons, each scenario says what to check, so the demo doubles as the checklist for the human sign-off:
  1. Nominal: the clock runs, the time controls work, Δv and fuel are in their places.
  2. Every sensor missing: NO SENSOR everywhere, Δv still shown.
  3. Sensors failed: NO SIGNAL.
  4. Three radar contacts at levels 0, 1 and 2.
  5. Alarms with an alert box.
  6. Launch burn at 1000x.
  7. Paused with no pulse left.
  8. A threat approaching from 240 to 3 km, animated: it moves steadily inward and the scope never shrinks.
  9. A T07 threat alert that clears when 1x is pressed.
  10. The self-destruct button.
  11. A live flight of T09's reference rocket through the real `FlightSimulation` (and T07's `EventDirector`). The instruments read live telemetry, and the debug panel shows the truth beside them, for example a true altitude of 399.7 km against 390.1 km on the noisy basic altimeter. Leaving the scenario or closing the demo ends the flight.

  Scenarios 1 to 10 use hand-made, fixed frames: only the clock and the next-pulse countdown move, so altitude and the other values stay constant on purpose.
- Tests: `tests/test_execution_console.gd`. On `main` at `fd4a558` (T07 merged) the full check passes.
- T07: the emergency overlay sits on its own `CanvasLayer` above this screen. T07's `THREAT_APPROACH` alert also appears in the console's alert box, as "THREAT APPROACHING".

- **Fuel and Δv swapped places** (Ilay's decision).
  - The bottom-centre panel the design gave to fuel shows Δv left (`frame.delta_v_remaining_meters_per_second`). Δv is not a sensor channel, so this panel never reads NO SENSOR.
  - The left column shows fuel in its first line, where the design had Δv. Fuel comes from the fuel-gauge channel, so it reads FUEL NO SENSOR / NO SIGNAL like the other instruments.
- Radar bearings are drawn as the telemetry gives them: inertial, 0 = +x (right), counter-clockwise. The frame carries no craft orientation, so the scope cannot be turned to "nose up".
- The radar scope range grows to fit the farthest contact, rounded up to 10/25/50/100/250/500/1000 km, and the outer ring is labelled. It never shrinks during a flight. A shrinking scope made an approaching contact jump outward: at 26 km it was drawn at 0.52 of the outer ring, and at 24 km at 0.96. The frame does not carry the radar's own range (see contract requests).
- **The alert also clears when the flight resumes**, not only on OK (Ilay's decision after the review). `raise_alert` always pauses, so a frame with a time scale above 0 means the alert was handled. Without this, the console kept showing "THREAT APPROACHING" after the player had answered T07's emergency overlay, which resumes at 1x.

## End-to-end run
T09's reference rocket (`ExampleFlight`) was flown through the real `Console` scene for 413 simulated seconds, at 1x, 100x and 1000x. The console updated from live frames with no script errors:
- acceleration 3.78 g during the launch burn and 0.00 g coasting
- altitude 215.3 km at T+185 s
- OVER TEMPERATURE listed after the burn
- NO SENSOR on every channel the rocket has no part for
- the time-scale highlight followed FlightSimulation's own drop to 1x before the pulse

## Found in other modules (not changed by T08)
- **T03:** during the launch burn `frame.seconds_until_next_pulse` is INF, so the clock reads "Next pulse in none" although a pulse is planned. `FlightSimulation._next_planned_pulse_time_seconds` returns INF until burnout creates the coast segment. `TelemetryFrame` documents INF as "none is left". The console shows what the frame says.
- **T03:** during the launch burn the craft stays on the pad. `FlightSimulation._advance_to` only moves the clock while a launch stage burns, and `_begin_next_launch_stage` then jumps to the burnout state. So the altimeter reads 0 km for the whole first-stage burn, then jumps to altitude. For the reference rocket the burn lasts more than 60 s. The acceleration and angle readings do change during the burn.
